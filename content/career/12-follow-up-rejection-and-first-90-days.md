## After every interview

Within 24 hours, send a short thank-you note:

```text
Hi Tanvir, thank you for your time today. I enjoyed hearing about
the migration to event-driven order processing; it's very close to
the outbox work I did at my current company. Looking forward to
the next steps.
```

Then update your tracker and write down the questions you were asked. They repeat across companies.

## Handling rejection

Rejection is normal. Even strong candidates get many "no"s.

1. Reply politely and ask: *"Is there any feedback that would help me improve?"*
2. Note which round you lost and why (coding speed, design depth, communication).
3. Add that gap to next week's study plan.
4. Ask to stay connected. Many people get hired by the same company 6 months later.

## The 30-60-90 day plan

Bring a short version to final interviews (this is the "next 6 months" plan from the [LinkedIn lesson](/learn/career/linkedin-interview-calls)), and a full version on your first day.

| Period | Goal | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Days 1–30: Learn** | Understand people, product, code | Set up local env, read architecture docs, ship a small fix, 1:1s with teammates |
| **Days 31–60: Contribute** | Own small features | Deliver a feature end to end, join on-call shadowing, improve one doc |
| **Days 61–90: Own** | Show impact | Lead a medium task, propose one improvement with data, agree goals for next quarter |

## Starting strong

- Ask your manager: *"How will you know I'm doing well after 3 months?"*
- Keep a **brag document**: a weekly list of what you shipped and learned. It makes reviews and your next job search much easier.
- Write down questions and answers as you learn; turn them into onboarding docs for the next person.
- Keep your LinkedIn and network warm. Your next opportunity often comes from the people you work with now.
