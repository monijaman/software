## Why behavioral rounds matter

Companies want to know how you work with people, handle pressure and learn from mistakes. Strong engineers are often rejected here because they ramble or give vague answers.

## The STAR structure

| Part | Share of answer | What to say |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **S**ituation | ~10% | Where, when, the context in 1–2 sentences |
| **T**ask | ~10% | Your responsibility or the goal |
| **A**ction | ~60% | What **you** did, step by step (say "I", not only "we") |
| **R**esult | ~20% | Outcome with numbers, and what you learned |

Keep each answer to about **2 minutes**.

### Example

*"Tell me about a time you handled a production incident."*

- **S**: During a sale our checkout API started timing out for 20% of users.
- **T**: I was on call and had to restore checkout quickly.
- **A**: I checked dashboards, found a slow query after a new release, rolled back, then added the missing index and a query-time alert.
- **R**: Checkout recovered in 18 minutes; the index cut query time from 2 s to 40 ms. We added a load test to the release checklist.

## Build a story bank

Prepare 6–8 stories that can answer many questions:

| Theme | Common questions |
| --- | --- |
| A hard technical problem | "Most challenging project?" |
| A failure or mistake | "Tell me about a time you failed." |
| A conflict | "Disagreement with a teammate or manager?" |
| Leadership / ownership | "When did you go beyond your role?" |
| Tight deadline | "How do you handle pressure?" |
| Learning fast | "Learned a new technology quickly?" |
| Feedback | "Received critical feedback?" |

## Common questions to rehearse

1. Tell me about yourself.
2. Why do you want to leave your current job?
3. Why this company?
4. What are your strengths and weaknesses?
5. Where do you see yourself in 3–5 years?

> 💡 For "weakness", name a real one you are actively fixing, and say how. Avoid fake weaknesses like "I work too hard."
