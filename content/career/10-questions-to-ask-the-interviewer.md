## Don't say "No, I'm good"

At the end of almost every interview you will hear: **"Do you have any questions for us?"** Many people skip it. That is a missed chance. Good questions show you are already thinking about doing the job well.

## 7 strong questions (copy and paste)

Pick the ones that fit the role:

1. "What does **success look like in the first 90 days** for this role?"
2. "What are the **top 2 priorities** you want this person to solve?"
3. "What are the **biggest challenges** the team is facing right now?"
4. "How do you **measure performance** for this role?"
5. "How is **reporting and communication** usually handled in the team?"
6. "What **tools and processes** do you currently use for tracking and documentation?"
7. "What would be the **next steps** after this interview, and the timeline?"

## How to use them

- **Don't ask all of them.** Pick **2–3** and ask with confidence.
- Listen to the answer and ask one follow-up. That turns it into a conversation.
- Write the answers down. They help you write your thank-you note and your 6-month plan.

## Match the question to the interviewer

| Interviewer | Good picks |
| --- | --- |
| Recruiter / HR | #7, team size, interview process |
| Hiring manager | #1, #2, #4 |
| Engineer / peer | #3, #6, "What does a normal week look like?" |
| Senior leader | "Where is the product going in the next year?" |

## Extra questions for developers

- "How does code go from a pull request to production?"
- "How much time goes to new features vs maintenance and on-call?"
- "How do you handle technical debt?"

## Avoid in the first interview

- Salary and leave questions before they bring it up (save for the recruiter or offer stage).
- Anything easily found on the company website.
- "Did I pass?"
