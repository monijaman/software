## Channels ranked by reply rate

From most to least effective, in most people's experience:

1. **Referral** from someone inside the company
2. **Direct message or email** to the hiring manager or recruiter
3. **Tailored application** on the company career page
4. **One-click "Easy Apply"**

Use all four, but spend most of your energy on the top two.

## Asking for a referral

Make it easy for the person to say yes. Send the job link, your resume and a 2-line summary they can forward.

```text
Hi Rahim, hope you're well! I'm applying for the Backend Engineer
role at your company (link). I have 3 years with Node.js and
PostgreSQL and built a payment service handling 2M requests/day.

Would you be comfortable referring me? I've attached my resume and
a short summary you can paste. No worries at all if not!
```

If you do not know anyone, engage with an employee's posts for a week or two first, then ask a question about their work before asking for a referral.

## Cold email to a hiring manager

```text
Subject: Backend Engineer – Node.js, payments experience

Hi Ms. Karim,

I saw Acme is hiring a Backend Engineer for the payments team.
At my current company I cut checkout failures by 32% by adding
idempotency keys and retries to our payment API.

I'd love to bring that to Acme. My CV and a 60-second video intro
are attached. Could we have a 15-minute call next week?

Best regards,
Name · LinkedIn · GitHub
```

Finding emails: company website, GitHub commits, conference talks, or tools like Hunter.io. Common formats are `first@company.com` or `first.last@company.com`.

## Follow-up rhythm

| Day | Action |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | Apply / send message |
| 5–7 | One polite follow-up |
| 14 | Final short check-in, then move on |

## Tracking sheet

Keep one sheet (Google Sheets, Notion or Trello) with columns: *Company, Role, Link, Date, Channel, Contact, Status, Next step, Notes*. Statuses: `Applied → Screening → Technical → Final → Offer / Rejected`.

Review it every week: which channel gives replies? Do more of that.
