## The idea

LinkedIn is not only a job board. Recruiters **search** it every day. Your goal is to be easy to find, easy to trust, and visible to the right people.

## 1. Build a complete "All-Star" profile

LinkedIn shows a profile strength meter. Fully complete profiles appear in far more recruiter searches than half-finished ones.

| Section | What good looks like |
| --- | --- |
| Photo | Clear face, plain background, smiling |
| Banner | Your stack or a one-line value statement |
| Headline | `Backend Engineer · Node.js · Go · AWS · I build reliable APIs` (not just "Student") |
| About | 4–6 lines: what you do, results, what you are looking for |
| Experience | Same impact bullets as your resume |
| Skills | 15–30 relevant skills, top 3 pinned |
| Featured | Best project, portfolio, an article |
| Open to Work | On, with exact job titles and locations (can be recruiters-only) |
| Custom URL | `linkedin.com/in/your-name` |

## 2. Grow a large, relevant network

A bigger network means your posts and profile reach more people. Many active job seekers aim for **thousands of connections** over time. On LinkedIn, a few thousand relevant connections can give you reach comparable to a much larger following on other platforms.

Connect with: engineers at target companies, tech recruiters, hiring managers, alumni, and people who post in your field. Quality beats random adds.

## 3. Have a solid job plan first

Decide the **industry, company type, role and the work you will do** before you start ([roadmap lesson](/learn/career/job-search-roadmap)). If you are unsure, sit with a mentor. LinkedIn works best when your profile and activity all point in one direction.

## 4. Research target companies

For each company you want:

- Follow the company page and read recent posts.
- Find who works there: team leads, engineers, recruiters.
- Check if anyone you know (or an alumnus) is there. That is your referral path.
- Follow those people and engage with their posts before you message them.

## 5. Apply fast and post regularly

Set **job alerts** for your exact titles. New posts appear on your feed and in alerts; when one matches, apply early (the first few days matter most).

Meanwhile, build authority by posting 1–3 times a week:

- What you learned this week (with a code snippet or diagram)
- A project walkthrough or bug story
- A short take on a tool in your stack

## 6. Message recruiters and insiders directly

Cold messages and emails work well when they are short and specific.

```text
Hi Ayesha, I saw the Backend Engineer opening at Acme. I've built
payment APIs in Node.js handling 2M requests/day (details on my
profile). Would you be open to a quick chat, or could you point me
to the right recruiter? Thank you!
```

Rules: under 80 words, mention the exact role, one proof point, one clear ask. Follow up once after 5–7 days.

## 7. Email your CV, and consider a video CV

"Easy Apply" gets hundreds of clicks. When you can find a recruiter's or hiring manager's email, **send your CV directly** with a short tailored note; it stands out more.

You can also attach a **60–90 second video CV** (a link to an unlisted YouTube or Loom video): introduce yourself, your best result, and why this role. It helps the recruiter put a face and voice to your name.

## 8. Document everything and prepare a plan

Track every application in a sheet:

| Company | Role | Date | Channel | Contact | Status | Next step |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Acme | Backend Eng | 3 Nov | Email to recruiter | Ayesha | Screening | Call on 10 Nov |

When a call comes, open your notes and prepare a short **"What I will do in the next 6 months"** plan for that role. Bringing it to the interview shows you have already thought like a team member.

> 💡 Follow these eight points consistently for a few weeks and LinkedIn becomes a steady source of calls. Share what works with your friends too.
